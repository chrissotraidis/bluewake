# Plan - 2026-09-18: the launch is authentic, the tail is the defect

**Why this document exists.** `REORGANIZATION_2026-09-17.md` named seven
workstreams and closed W0-W4 on measured evidence. The work landed: the chassis
schedule, the 16,384-cycle window and the emitter's static pc-resumption table
took the play-window median from 11.30 to ~33 fps and the headless route from
158.68 s to 70.44 s, with the route digest `UNCHANGED` throughout. That plan
ended on one open question and one honest negative:

> whether the product passes is decided by how much the renderer adds rather
> than by the simulation. That is the question an idle host would answer in one
> run.

This document answers that question, and the answer changes what is left.

## 1. The measurement nobody had taken

Every performance number the loop has collected since W1 came from
`BLUEWAKE_RENDERER=headless`, which submits no GX, creates no Metal pipeline and
presents nothing. The signed app does all three. The last rendered figure in the
tree was a comment in `scripts/app_acceptance_test.sh` ("about 120 ms per
retrace") written before W1 and W4.

The rendered route now runs unattended through the pad layer - no key focus, so
no idle host is needed - and the same 14,100-retrace route was measured both
ways on the same build, composite and card, at the shipping defaults (chassis
schedule, 16,384-cycle window).

| play-window (retrace 13,910..14,100) | headless | **rendered** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| median fps | 33.22 | **33.58** |
| p99 frame time | 47.34 ms | **77.63 ms** |
| mean fps | 30.70 | **24.39** |
| frame time, min .. max | 1.86 .. 48.78 ms | **1.86 .. 78.10 ms** |
| route wall | 70.44 s | **242.50 s** |
| host CPU (user + sys) | - | **109.1 s of 242.5 s** |
| real speed (guest / wall) | 333.6% | **96.9%** |
| peak RSS | 242.5 MB | **774.1 MB** |
| host turns | 32,203,791 | **32,203,791** |
| final pc / stop | `0x80307EF4` normal | **`0x80307EF4` normal** |
| route digest | `92dd816c...` | **UNCHANGED** |

The rendered run is the shipped path: Aurora window, Metal, the audio device,
`vsync = true`. It stops normally at the same pc after the same 32,203,791 host
turns, so it is the same route and not a near-miss.

## 2. What it means

**The renderer is not the throughput problem.** The play-window median is
33.58 fps rendered against 33.22 headless - a difference of 0.36 fps, inside the
noise. Presenting the game costs essentially nothing at the median. What it
costs is in the tail: p99 goes from 47.34 ms to 77.63 ms, and the mean falls
from 30.70 to 24.39 fps. The whole-frame figure moved 0.9 ms; the worst-frame
figure moved 30 ms.

**The app is real-time paced, and that is correct.** Retraces 1..13,850 advance
at 16.787 ms each - 59.6 Hz, sixty retraces per second, one retrace per unit of
guest time. The run takes 242.5 s of wall for 235.0 s of authored content, 96.9%
of real time, while spending only 109 s of CPU. The pacing is the audio queue:
`aurora_audio.cpp` throttles with `SDL_Delay(1)` once the queued PCM exceeds
`g_audio_max_queue_ms`, so the emulator is clocked by the device that plays the
sound, with vsync on top. `BLUEWAKE_RENDERER=headless` removes the pacer, which
is exactly why the bench reports 333.6% of real time and the app reports 103%.
The bench number is a throughput measurement in a regime the product never
enters.

**TTP-B is unreachable by construction, and this is arithmetic, not
engineering.** The cold new game admits control at retrace **20,256** - the
guest's own `[player-milestone] control-admitted` signal, measured, and later
than the 20,044 that `REORGANIZATION_2026-09-17.md` quotes - which at an
authentic 60 Hz is **337.6 s of authored content**. A paced application cannot
present that content in less wall time than it takes to play, at any emulator
speed, fast or slow. The 300 s bar is therefore failed at 100% emulated speed
by 37.6 s, and the only way to move the number is to stop playing the content.

This is not a new discovery - the 09-17 plan says so in its W5 section - but it
has now been measured on the rendered path rather than inferred, and the
renderer is confirmed not to be what rescues or damns it.

**What feels terrible is the tail, not the average.** A median frame of 29.78 ms
is 33.6 fps against a 30 fps baseline - fine. But p99 is 77.63 ms, 2.6x the
median, and the mean sits 37% above the median. That is a visible hitch roughly
every hundredth frame, and it is worse rendered than headless by 30 ms. Nothing
in the W1-W4 workstream was aimed at it, because every instrument in that
workstream was headless and the tail is the part the headless measurement
cannot see.

**Peak RSS triples rendered.** 242.5 MB becomes 774.1 MB. That is Metal and the
GPU residency, it is not obviously a defect, and it is now the number the soak
and the iOS port will be read against.

### M6 - Give the renderer a measurable tier (new, 2026-09-18)

**Done, 2026-09-18.** `scripts/profile_play.sh` + `scripts/sample_owners.py` sample the rendered route
during the live play window and report owner shares. Shares are self-normalising against machine load and
reproduce to 0.6-0.9 points across runs, so a 4-point renderer effect is now resolvable where wall-clock
fps could not see anything under ~20%. The tool's header carries the one mistake it was built after: a
parent's share includes its children, and subtracting them is not a self time.

With that instrument the first renderer candidate was measured and retracted - the count-only assembly
walk is real dead work but costs well under one percent, not the four the subtraction suggested (see
`CURRENT.md`). The 18% GX owner is now measurable; what is not yet known is whether any of it is
removable.

`scripts/bench.sh` runs headless, and headless never executes the Aurora configuration path, so **every
renderer-side change is invisible to the project's primary gate** - including the 18.3% GX translation
the M3 profile found and the 4.1% count-only assembly walk recorded in `CURRENT.md`. The rendered tier
(`scripts/play.sh --timing --route-full`) exists and works, but its live-window mean has ranged from
33.6% to 41.0% of authentic across runs, which is wider than most effects worth chasing.

Either the rendered tier needs repeats and a stated resolution, or the renderer's work needs to be
attributable from a headless run. Until one of those is true, the 18% GX owner cannot be improved on
evidence, and the loop would be making renderer changes it cannot validate.

## 4. The plan

### M1 - Make the route drivable without the user's desk (do first)

**Done, 2026-09-18.** `scripts/play.sh --route-full` drives the whole route, headless or rendered,
from launch to `[player-milestone] control-admitted` at retrace 20,256, with the accepted route's
milestones unchanged. The mechanism is `BLUEWAKE_PAD_SCRIPT="retrace:buttons:length,..."`, the general
form of the three fixed pulses. Two negative results along the way are recorded in `CURRENT.md`: the
cutscene's event is 38 and not 29 (the guest's own event table names it `awake`), and the
`0x803CA7D2` message-status gate - single-event or generic - never fires for it, so a scheduled press
list is what actually walks it. What follows is the plan as it was written, kept because the reasoning
is what the implementation followed.

**This is the throughput fix, and it unblocks M2 and half of M3.** Half the
loop's verification needs the app to hold the key window, and macOS 26 refuses
`activateFromApplication:` while another application has focus, so acceptance,
the save artifact and the reload test all wait on the user not using their own
Mac. The 09-17 plan called this W6 and nominated three routes; the cheapest is
now proven, because the run above drove the whole 14,100-retrace route through
the pad layer, unattended, with no focus at all.

The missing piece is exactly one thing: there are only three scheduled presses
(`BLUEWAKE_PAD_PULSE_RETRACE/LENGTH`, `PULSE2_*`, `PULSE3_*`), and walking
title -> file select -> new game -> name entry -> intro -> Outset -> control
needs about a dozen. Generalise the three into a list - one env var of
`retrace:buttons:length` entries - and the full route becomes drivable.

Then, explicitly: a pad-driven run is a *measurement*, not *evidence*. The
distinction the loop already draws between a route driver and a human press is
the right one, `app_acceptance_test.sh` keeps its real-key default, and a pad
mode is labelled as what it is: the number, not the proof.

### M2 - Settle TTP-B with the save-continue path

`REORGANIZATION_2026-09-17.md` W5 and its decision 2 both land here. The PRD
forbids a save-state warm start; it does not forbid continuing from a save the
game itself wrote, which is the game's own persistence. Boot -> title -> file
select -> load -> control is roughly 100 s, comfortably inside the bar, and it
is the same P4 milestone 9 the compatibility campaign needs anyway (`write`
proven, quit and reload open).

This needs a user decision - is the bar read against a cold new game, which
arithmetic makes unreachable, or against a human reaching controllable Outset
gameplay, which the clause's own wording says - but the engineering to support
the second answer should not wait for it.

### M3 - Profile the rendered play-scene tail

**Done, 2026-09-18** (`CURRENT.md`, "the rendered play scene is profiled"). A 25-second `sample` of the
shipping rendered path during live Outset play. The main thread is 87.9% translated dispatch and 7.8%
host device service; **Aurora's `render_worker` is idle in `BoundedQueue::pop_for` for 94% of the
samples**, so the GPU is not the bottleneck. Inside the dispatch, one guest function spends 16-18% of the
whole frame inside `host_mmio_write` -> `aurora_backend_gx_write` -> `shadow_frontend_write` ->
`RetailGxFrontend::flush` -> `emit_new_packets` -> `GXCoreSink::submit_packet` -> `accumulate_assembly`:
synchronous CPU-side GX translation, planning and assembly on the main thread. Rendered live play
measures **33.6% of authentic speed** over 790 retraces (13.167 s guest in 39.238 s wall; median
37.85 ms per retrace against an authentic 16.667 ms).

Start with an instrument, not a change, per the discipline that has served this
workstream. The headless profile assigned 70.8% of a frame to translated bodies
and found no single large item; the rendered profile has never been taken, and
it is the only one that can see the 30 ms of tail the renderer adds - GPU
stalls, pipeline creation, texture upload, a per-frame readback, or a
presentation wait.

The frame-timing instrument already exists and stamps wall time per retrace, so
the profile is one run of the path M1 makes drivable, plus attributing the p99
frames specifically rather than averaging them into a median that hides them.

### M4 - Loop economics (cheap, independent, do alongside)

**Largely measured, 2026-09-18, and it is worse than this section assumed.** The composite is 748
translation units from 489 MB of generated C; the single hottest chunk takes 38 s to compile, a
DOL-only recompile of 206 objects takes **99 minutes** at 8 workers, and a full rebuild is hours. That
is the actual reason iterations are slow, and it is now priced. Two things follow: the DOL-only path
(recompile the DOL chunks, relink in ~6 s, bench) makes emitter work on the play scene's hot code
affordable at ~100 min per iteration, and the *other* half - that a single bench run cannot resolve an
effect under ~5% - means small wins need repeats rather than runs.

One flag class is already closed: `-fstack-check -stack-protector 1 -mdarwin-stkchk-strong-link`
produce byte-identical `__text` on the hottest chunk, and `-O3` on the DOL chunks is 3-7% worse on route
wall with no play-window gain (both in `CURRENT.md`).

Three items, each small, each paid back per iteration:

* **The defaults drifted and nobody noticed.** `scripts/play.sh` pinned
  `BLUEWAKE_CYCLE_CAP=dynamic` - the policy commit `8da70d2` adopted and commit
  `be84bdf` superseded - under a comment claiming it matched the benchmark. The
  human play path was running the slower policy the shipping default had
  already replaced. Fixed in this commit; the runtime's `main.c` still carries
  the superseded comment above the real one and should lose it.
* **`CURRENT.md` is 758 KB** and every agent reads it before doing anything, so
  its sprawl is charged on every iteration. Make it a rolling window with a
  hard size and move durable state into the small files that already exist.
* **Build cost.** A runtime `.c` edit costs ~10 minutes and a header edit
  poisons 756 objects. `ccache` and a unity build are both unmeasured here and
  both cheap to measure.

### M5 - NFR-001, only after M3

**First landed increment, 2026-09-18.** Patch `patches/dolrecomp/0017` collapses the duplicated
block-leader guard: **+2.8% on the live-window median** (33.75 against 32.84, three runs against four,
no overlap), digest UNCHANGED, the composite 858,624 bytes smaller. It cost a 89.7-minute DOL-only
rebuild. Against the 1.81x the play scene needs, that is roughly 2.8% of a 81% gap - about 29 more
increments of this size, or 45 hours of rebuilding - which is the realistic cost of this workstream and
the reason it should be scheduled rather than assumed.

**Second increment, 2026-09-18: neutral, reverted.** Two trims in the cycle-observation machinery -
the reconcile using its compile-time suffix instead of reloading the field twice, and dropping the
guard's redundant pc store - measured nothing (33.66 against 33.75 on the live-window median) and grew
the composite 6.5%, so they are out of the tree and the artifact is byte-identical to the 0017 build.
Two rebuilds (152 and 90 minutes) bought two facts worth having: **the observation bookkeeping is not
the bottleneck**, despite standing on every memory instruction; and **a small emitter change can move
the binary by 6.5% in a direction the source does not predict**, so a per-increment size check before
the benchmark is cheap insurance. Both are recorded in `CURRENT.md`.

The emulated-cycle rate is ~53% of authentic (259M cycles/s against 486 MHz) and
it needs structural work in the emitter, not a targeted fix. W4 closed with the
statement that the identified pieces - 17% envelope, ~18% inlined access path,
4.3% guard bodies, 1% FP helper - do not sum to the 1.81x the play scene needs.

**That 1.81x is still outstanding, and M5 is therefore a live workstream rather
than a closed one.** The measured play scene is at 51.3% of authentic speed
headless and 40.6% rendered (see the correction entry in `CURRENT.md`), which is
1.81x and 2.47x short respectively. W4's arithmetic was right and its closing
paragraph - "the play scene is at 29.92 fps against the PRD's 30 fps baseline,
99.7% of it" - compared a retrace rate against a game-frame rate and is wrong
by a factor of two. So M5 is the largest remaining item, not a footnote, and it
should be entered from a rendered profile (M3) with the target restated in the
benchmark's own units: retraces per wall second, against 60.

## 5. What not to do

Do not treat the headless bench as the product. It reports 333.6% of real speed
in a regime the packaged app never enters, and it is blind to the entire tail.
Do not re-derive TTP-B as an engineering problem before the definition is
settled; it is arithmetic, and 334.1 s of content does not fit in 300 s. Do not
weaken a clause to make a log pass. Do not run a timing measurement while a
build is running - the numbers above were taken on an otherwise quiet machine.

## Correction, 2026-09-18: W4 shares are code size, not dynamic work

The M5 section prices the remaining work against W4 attribution (about 40 percent of the emitted code). The first screening-loop candidate tested that and it does not hold: dropping the redundant mirror-bit clause from get_ram_ptr fast path removes about a fifth of the access path and moved the instruction stream 0.16 percent on 26 percent of the window, so about 0.6 percent overall, against the 3.6 percent the 18 percent share implies. Digest UNCHANGED, so it was equivalent and simply not worth anything.

So the access path is about 3 percent of the dynamic stream and the identified pieces do not add up to the 1.65x this plan claimed. Do not plan against W4 percentages. The remainder needs a structurally leaner emitter, and the instrument worth building next is a dynamic breakdown of where the 491.7M instructions per play retrace go.


## Next candidates, ranked from the dynamic census instead of from code size

Every earlier ranking in this plan used W4 s code-size shares, and the first one measured that way (the
inlined access path, 18% of the emitted code) was worth 0.6%. These are ranked from the dynamic table in
CURRENT.md - the rendered play window at uniform IPC - and each carries the cost of finding out.

1. **The GX FIFO write path, ~17-20% of the rendered window, all of it synchronous on the main thread
   while the render worker sits idle 94% of the time.** The largest dynamic owner that is not a long tail.
   It is host code, so a rebuild is about fifteen seconds and the rendered instrument resolves a share in
   about five minutes - the fastest iteration in the project. The question to answer first is not how to
   parallelise it but *what inside it is removable*: emit_new_packets is 14.5% and submit_packet 14.0%, so
   almost all of it is real packet and draw construction, not overhead. The two overhead-shaped items
   already measured are flush s own work (2.6%) and parse_stream (2.3%).
2. **The device sync at 6.4% and the edge service at 4.3%.** Both host side, both cheap to iterate, both
   individually small. Inside them the pc lookup is 2.2% and the card intercept test 1.3%, which is what a
   trim there is worth - fractions of a percent.
3. **The guest long tail.** After two functions nothing is above 2% and the top twenty chunks are 30% of
   the window, so this is only reachable by making every emitted instruction cheaper. That is the redesign,
   and the dynamic numbers say it has to be broad rather than targeted.

What this ordering does not change: the target needs 1.81x and nothing on the list is of that size. The
honest reading is that item 3 is the only one that could be, it is a redesign, and items 1 and 2 are where a
bounded, fast-iteration gain might still be found.

## Where the goal stands, one page (2026-09-18, end of stretch)

Written because the ledger has grown past the point where a reader can see the position from it.

What works. The retail GZLE01 DOL and all 415 RELs boot into controllable Outset gameplay on the macOS
product path, from the signed app, with real keys, real video, real audio, the game own save and a normal
stop. The launch runs at authentic speed, paced by the audio queue. The route digest is the correctness gate
and has stayed green through every change that shipped.

What does not. The play scene is at 51 percent of authentic speed headless and 41 percent rendered. It needs
1.81x. It is instruction-bound at IPC about 4.0, so only fewer instructions buys speed, and the numbers
inside the certified route are:

| | instructions per play retrace |
| --- | --- |
| headless | 302.3 M |
| rendered | 403.3 M |
| renderer share | +101.0 M (+33.4 percent) |

Why trimming cannot get there. Every candidate tried was measured, and every one was small or zero: the
inlined access-path clause 0.6 percent, the GX sink per-draw payload copy 0.07 percent, the
cycle-observation bookkeeping under 1 percent, compiler flags refuted, stack-hardening byte-identical, the
host not stalled. W4 attribution turned out to be code size rather than dynamic work - an 18 percent share
bought 0.6 percent - and the emitted code is flat, 11 to 24 lines per guest instruction with about ten lines
of envelope as the floor under everything. The renderer 33 percent is dominated by GX packet and draw
construction, whose obvious overheads are the two refuted items, so what remains is work the renderer needs.

The one change that landed is patch 0017, the duplicated block-leader guard, at +2.8 percent on the play
window. The two bugs found along the way matter more than the percentage: the emitted pc-resumption table
that was rebuilt on the stack at every call, and patch 0050, a fatal WGSL codegen bug that was aborting every
rendered run.

What would change the answer. Either a structurally leaner emitter - fewer host instructions per guest
instruction across the board, which is a redesign measured in weeks and is the only path to 1.81x - or
accepting the current authentic launch and a lower play-scene target. The PRD puts that choice above this
loop.

The other decision. TTP-B: Link takes control at retrace 20,256, which is 337.6 s of authored content at an
authentic 60 Hz, so the five-minute cold-new-game bar is failed by 37.6 s at 100 percent emulated speed.
Continuing from a save the game itself wrote fits inside the bar and is staged; a save-state warm start is
what the PRD forbids.

How to work here now. Measure before building: bench_instructions.sh resolves 0.02 percent of the instruction
stream in three minutes, bench_rendered.sh gives the rendered split with a guard that refuses aborted runs,
and recomp_chunks.sh screens a candidate on the hot ten in about twenty minutes against a ninety-minute full
DOL rebuild. Verify the run before reading its number - eight measurements in this stretch needed correcting
and every one was caught that way. Never git checkout a file in ref/; its uncommitted state is the patch
series.
